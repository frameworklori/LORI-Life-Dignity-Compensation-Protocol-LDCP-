# LDCP Operational Workflow

## Workflow Overview

```text
Incident occurs
-> Immediate dignity response
-> Evidence preservation
-> ODRAF risk and responsibility review
-> Jury or review panel packet
-> Safety reform plan
-> Compensation recommendation
-> Appeal window
-> Follow-up care
-> Transparency disclosure
-> Drift check
```

## Step 1: Incident Intake

Responsible role: incident coordinator.

Inputs:

- Incident date and location
- Harm type
- Known facts
- Immediate family or affected-person needs
- Urgent safety concerns

Outputs:

- Case ID
- Initial severity category
- Family liaison assignment
- Evidence hold notice

## Step 2: Immediate Dignity Response

Responsible role: family liaison.

Actions:

- Contact family or affected person with care.
- Provide emergency assistance information.
- Offer funeral, travel, translation, counseling, or immediate support where relevant.
- Explain that urgent support does not erase legal rights.

Outputs:

- Support record
- Consent and communication preferences
- Follow-up appointment

## Step 3: Evidence Preservation

Responsible role: evidence custodian.

Actions:

- Preserve logs, records, witness statements, footage, medical documents, system traces, or decision records.
- Freeze deletion schedules where lawful.
- Record chain of custody.

Outputs:

- Evidence inventory
- Missing evidence list
- Privacy classification

## Step 4: ODRAF Review

Responsible role: risk reviewer.

Actions:

- Assess harm severity.
- Assess preventability.
- Assess institutional responsibility.
- Assess irreversibility and dependency impact.
- Identify uncertainty and evidence gaps.

Outputs:

- ODRAF profile
- Risk-weight summary
- Evidence guardrail report

## Step 5: Review Panel

Responsible role: jury chair or panel coordinator.

Actions:

- Prepare decision packet.
- Review evidence and ODRAF summary.
- Vote or deliberate according to governance rules.
- Record dissent or uncertainty.

Outputs:

- Verdict
- Responsibility level
- Review notes
- Appeal eligibility

## Step 6: Safety Reform Plan

Responsible role: safety reform owner.

Actions:

- Identify root causes.
- Define corrective actions.
- Assign deadlines.
- Define verification method.
- Decide what can be publicly disclosed.

Outputs:

- Safety reform plan
- Implementation owner
- Disclosure schedule

## Step 7: Compensation Recommendation

Responsible role: compensation reviewer.

Inputs:

- Verdict
- Family impact profile
- Dependency analysis
- Lost support estimate
- Care burden
- Emotional harm acknowledgment
- Affordability and anti-abuse checks

Outputs:

- Compensation category
- Compensation form
- Payment or support schedule
- Non-monetary restitution commitments

## Step 8: Appeal Window

Responsible role: appeals officer.

Actions:

- Notify affected family or person of appeal rights.
- Accept corrections or new evidence.
- Review procedural fairness.
- Escalate to independent review if needed.

Outputs:

- Appeal decision
- Revised packet if applicable

## Step 9: Follow-Up Care

Responsible role: family liaison.

Actions:

- Conduct scheduled follow-up.
- Confirm whether support was delivered.
- Record unresolved needs.
- Avoid intrusive contact if family declines.

Outputs:

- Follow-up log
- Open support items

## Step 10: Transparency Disclosure

Responsible role: transparency owner.

Actions:

- Publish or provide safety reform completion report.
- Avoid exposing private family details.
- State what changed and why.

Outputs:

- Transparency statement
- Completed reform record

## Step 11: Drift Check

Responsible role: audit owner.

Actions:

- Check whether safety reforms remain active.
- Review repeat incidents.
- Review compensation fairness metrics.
- Identify process drift.

Outputs:

- Drift report
- Improvement backlog

## Minimum Records

Every LDCP case should produce:

- Case intake
- Immediate support record
- Evidence inventory
- ODRAF profile
- Review panel decision
- Safety reform plan
- Compensation recommendation
- Follow-up log
- Transparency record
- Drift check

## Ethical Rule

No compensation process should begin by asking what a life is worth.

LDCP begins by asking what dignity, responsibility, prevention, and family impact require.
