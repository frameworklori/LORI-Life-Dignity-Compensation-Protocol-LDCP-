# LDCP Feasibility Plan

## Purpose

This document translates LDCP from an ethical protocol into a feasible institutional implementation plan.

LDCP can be implemented as a governance process, not as a replacement for courts, insurance, regulators, or legal counsel.

## Feasibility Conclusion

LDCP is feasible as a staged institutional protocol if it is deployed with the following boundaries:

- It is voluntary or contractually adopted.
- It does not waive legal rights unless reviewed separately by counsel.
- It does not pressure families into settlement.
- It separates urgent dignity support from final compensation.
- It includes independent review and appeal.
- It publishes safety reforms after incident review.

The strongest early use case is not national law. The strongest early use case is an organizational pilot inside a company, hospital network, platform, insurer, or public agency.

## Implementation Layers

### Layer 1: Immediate Response

Goal: protect dignity in the first hours and days after severe harm.

Operational requirements:

- Emergency contact protocol
- Family liaison assignment
- Funeral and travel support rules
- Short-term emergency fund
- Written statement of support
- Non-admission legal boundary where required

Feasibility: high.

Reason: organizations already maintain crisis response and insurance workflows. LDCP adds ethical order and family-centered structure.

### Layer 2: Accountability Review

Goal: identify responsibility, causation, and institutional failure without hiding behind procedural delay.

Operational requirements:

- Incident record
- Evidence preservation
- ODRAF profile
- Internal and independent reviewers
- Timeline of known facts
- Public or family-facing explanation

Feasibility: medium-high.

Reason: many institutions already conduct incident reviews, but LDCP requires more transparency and moral framing.

### Layer 3: Safety Reform

Goal: prevent compensation from becoming a substitute for prevention.

Operational requirements:

- Root-cause analysis
- Corrective action plan
- Completion deadline
- Public improvement disclosure
- Drift check after implementation

Feasibility: high if linked to compliance or risk management.

Reason: safety improvement processes already exist in healthcare, aviation, workplace safety, public infrastructure, and software governance.

### Layer 4: Family-Based Compensation

Goal: compensate according to real family impact rather than only legal minimums or corporate cost control.

Operational requirements:

- Family dependency profile
- Lost support estimate
- Care burden assessment
- Emotional harm acknowledgment
- Long-term education, housing, healthcare, or livelihood support options
- Anti-abuse and affordability review

Feasibility: medium.

Reason: compensation can be implemented, but it requires careful legal, actuarial, and ethical review.

### Layer 5: Follow-Up and Transparency

Goal: preserve long-term dignity and institutional trust.

Operational requirements:

- Follow-up schedule
- Family contact consent
- Safety reform progress disclosure
- Review closure report
- Public accountability summary where appropriate

Feasibility: medium-high.

Reason: the process is administratively simple but requires cultural commitment.

## Minimum Viable Protocol

An organization can pilot LDCP with six documents:

1. Incident intake form
2. Family support checklist
3. Evidence and responsibility worksheet
4. Jury or review panel decision packet
5. Compensation recommendation worksheet
6. Safety reform and follow-up report

## Required Roles

- Family liaison
- Incident coordinator
- Evidence custodian
- Independent reviewer
- Compensation reviewer
- Safety reform owner
- Appeals officer
- Privacy officer

In smaller organizations, one person may hold more than one role, but evidence custody and appeal review should remain independent.

## Data Requirements

LDCP should collect only what is necessary.

Core data:

- Incident type
- Date and location
- Affected person or family relationship profile
- Immediate needs
- Evidence summary
- Responsibility assessment
- Safety failure classification
- Compensation category
- Follow-up status

Sensitive data should be minimized, time-limited, and access-controlled.

## Risk Controls

Potential misuse:

- Using LDCP to reduce legal exposure
- Offering dignity funds in exchange for silence
- Over-standardizing human grief
- Under-compensating through rigid formulas
- Making public statements before facts are known
- Creating appeal processes that are only symbolic

Controls:

- Independent review
- Plain-language family rights notice
- Separation of emergency support from final settlement
- Appeal path
- Public safety reform disclosure
- Audit KPIs

## Success Metrics

Operational metrics:

- Time to first family contact
- Time to emergency support
- Evidence preservation completeness
- Time to accountability statement
- Time to safety reform plan
- Safety reform completion rate
- Appeal rate

Dignity metrics:

- Family understanding of process
- Family ability to submit corrections
- Follow-up completion rate
- Transparency report completion

System metrics:

- Repeat incident reduction
- Safety reform drift rate
- Compensation fairness review score
- Independent reviewer variance

## Recommended Pilot Scope

Start with historical or synthetic cases.

Do not begin with active fatality cases until:

- Legal review is complete
- Privacy workflow is approved
- Family liaison training is done
- Appeal process is tested
- Compensation authority is defined

## Practical Verdict

LDCP is feasible as an institutional ethical response protocol.

It is not immediately feasible as a universal legal standard without jurisdiction-specific work.

The best path is:

1. Internal pilot
2. Independent review
3. Limited real-world adoption
4. Public reporting template
5. Policy partnership
